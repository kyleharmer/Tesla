2. Dealer interaction

“I'm interested in the red 2021 Model 3. The price and mileage caught my attention. Before we get too far, what can you tell me about the car and its history?”
If they do NOT disclose rebuilt history
“Any accidents, salvage history, rebuilt title, or other title history I should know about?” If they still say no: “Okay. I pulled an NMVTIS-based report on the VIN, and it shows an Ohio salvage title after a rear-end collision and a Michigan rebuilt title. Can you walk me through that?”
If they DO disclose it
“I appreciate you telling me. I pulled the history too, so I've seen the Copart information. I'm not automatically scared off by a rebuilt car if I understand what happened. What exactly was replaced?”

3. In-car / Tesla touchscreen

Tesla-specific touchscreen checks

① Confirm VIN + odometer
  1. Tap Controls.
  2. Open Software.
  3. Confirm the VIN is 5YJ3E1EA2MF052825.
  4. Photograph this screen and the current mileage.
② Enter Tesla Service Mode
  1. Park the vehicle.
  2. Go to Controls → Software.
  3. Find the large vehicle/model identification area.
  4. Press and hold the large word MODEL / model badge for about 2 seconds, then release.
  5. An access-code keyboard should appear.
  6. Type service.
  7. Tap OK.
  8. The screen should show a red Service Mode border/overlay.
Service Mode itself is an official Tesla diagnostic interface. Browsing information is useful. Do not randomly run component actuations, calibrations, resets or repair procedures.
③ Check Service Mode alerts
  1. Enter Service Mode.
  2. Look for the Alerts / vehicle health section.
  3. Look especially for codes beginning with BMS, which relate to the high-voltage battery management system.
  4. Also pay attention to restraint/airbag, charging, thermal-system, drive-unit, camera and low-voltage faults.
  5. Take photos of anything active or recently recurring.
Do not assume every historic alert is a current failure. What matters most is an active/repeating alert, especially battery, thermal, restraint or drivetrain related.
④ Check Tesla Service History — especially useful for the mileage mystery
  1. Enter Service Mode.
  2. Open Service History if present.
  3. Look for dated service entries.
  4. Check whether entries show the odometer at the time of service.
  5. Photograph any useful entries.
This could be extremely useful. Example: a Tesla service entry at 2,500 miles in 2022 followed by the 4,861-mile Copart record in 2023 strongly supports the low-mileage story.
Caution: Service History is not necessarily complete. Some maintenance records can be entered by owners or independent repairers, so treat it as corroborating evidence, not absolute proof.
⑤ Check Battery Health
  1. Exit Service Mode if necessary and return to the normal interface.
  2. Try Controls → Service → Battery Health.
  3. If the feature exists, read the current battery-health evaluation.
  4. If a prior Battery Health Test result is displayed, photograph it.
Do NOT start a new Battery Health Test at the dealer. Tesla says the full test can take up to 24 hours, requires AC charging and specific battery-state conditions, and deliberately discharges/recharges the pack.
⑥ Cameras
  1. Put the car in Reverse and inspect the rear camera image.
  2. Activate the left turn signal and check the left repeater-camera view.
  3. Activate the right signal and check the right repeater-camera view.
  4. Look for black screens, flicker, distorted image, warnings or calibration errors.
  5. If available in the Service menu, use the camera preview only to inspect image availability—do not start calibrations.
This is particularly useful here because the auction photographs made the front-fender repeater-camera area look unusual.
⑦ Charging status
  1. Open Controls → Charging.
  2. Confirm the charge port responds normally.
  3. Look for charging warnings.
  4. Ask the dealer whether the vehicle currently DC fast-charges / Supercharges normally.
  5. If they claim yes, ask whether they have recently demonstrated it.
⑧ Exit Service Mode
  1. Open the Service Mode menu.
  2. Tap Exit Service Mode near the top of the screen.
  3. Confirm the red Service Mode border/overlay disappears.
